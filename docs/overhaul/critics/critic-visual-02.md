## Verdict

The actual captures communicate a bright, stylized naval game, but they are **not close to the targets' theatrical cinematic anime quality**. The strongest alignment is the cobalt/turquoise/cream palette, expansive sea, recognizable hero-ship color blocking, and readable ship silhouettes. The dominant gap is physical authorship: the targets have layered construction, purposeful crew poses, directional light, convincing contact, and inhabited landscapes; the captures expose simple geometry and largely uniform shading. This is a substantial visual gap, not a final-polish gap.

Exact camera positions or copied reference details are unnecessary. A usable gameplay camera can still deliver the targets' dimensionality, material separation, landmark richness, and sense of weight.

- **Form:** hulls and sails identify the ships, but rails, ports, mast hardware, deck furniture, and architecture lack layered construction. The closeup exposes crew as capsule-like figures with little articulated anatomy or readable work pose. The arch's clean ring profile and nearly uniform thickness read as constructed geometry rather than monumental geology.
- **Material:** the targets distinguish worn painted timber, canvas, dark iron, rope, foliage, and rock even at medium distance. Actual timber uses prominent flowing grain across large flat surfaces; blue windows, colored roofs, sails, and pale cliffs have similarly simple responses. Texture frequency does not compensate for missing seams, bevels, cavities, and material boundaries.
- **Light:** cream sails, cream cliffs, and white trim are bright, but convincing directional cast shadows and contact darkness are scarce. Crew and cannons do not feel firmly seated on the deck. The roof highlight appears graphic rather than part of a coherent light response. Warm sunlight against cool shaded surfaces would add much more depth.
- **Composition:** calm sailing is usable, but the center sail hides most of the deck and the arch is cropped into a pale wall. Broadside brings the ships close enough for useful inspection, but their silhouettes crowd each other and the opponent leaves the frame. Crew closeup allocates much of the image to a cropped sail and oversized cabin roof; the visible people are a secondary cluster rather than the subject.
- **Readability:** player/target HUD grouping and battery states are apparent. At this output size, secondary labels and the central control hint are extremely small. Broadside projectiles are dark beads with faint trails over a busy sea; muzzle origin, intended target, and hit event are much less legible than in the combat concept. The crew closeup does not visually explain specific jobs.

## Five highest-impact bounded changes

1. **Resolve one hero ship and three visible work stations as finished assets.** Keep the existing recognizable silhouette, but give its rail real balusters and thickness, cannons carriages and recessed mouths, the deck discrete plank joints, and rigging visible attachment hardware. Replace the most exposed crew with articulated silhouettes at helm, gun, and repair stations, each with hands visibly meeting a task object. Start with the areas visible from the normal sailing, broadside, and deck cameras. **Acceptance:** the same three views read as a constructed ship occupied by people doing identifiable work, including with surface textures temporarily simplified.

2. **Establish one coherent sunny lighting and material pass.** Add readable sail/mast shadows across the deck, contact shadows under feet and cannon wheels, shaded rail cavities, cool sides under warm lit tops, and distinct responses for canvas, painted wood, iron, glass, and foliage. Reduce the oversized swirling wood-grain pattern; use plank structure and restrained wear. Preserve clear silhouettes instead of outlining every small part equally. **Acceptance:** a closeup and medium shot have visible depth without relying on saturated flat color or a large repeated texture, and pale cliffs retain shaded structure rather than appearing self-lit.

3. **Give moving ships a convincing waterline and wake.** Prioritize a narrow irregular contact band, bow displacement, separated spray accents, and a trailing wake that belongs to each hull. Current broad sea undulations work as a base, but bright foam appears detached in places and the hero ship has little sense of displacing water. Compose foam as varied thin crest ribbons and localized breaking patches; avoid large blurred foreground blobs. **Acceptance:** hulls look embedded in the sea in all three views, and speed/direction are inferable from hull interaction rather than only the speed number. Motion continuity still requires runtime inspection.

4. **Finish one landmark corridor around the arch.** Break the regular ring silhouette into asymmetric geological masses, add a stepped cliff base and shaded recesses, and place clustered vegetation with a small legible settlement or harbor at its foot. Use foreground rock, middle-distance coast, and cooler distant land to establish scale. Do not scatter equal-detail objects everywhere. **Acceptance:** an ordinary sailing approach reveals an inhabited destination and geological depth; the landmark remains recognizable at gameplay distance without needing a special matching reference camera.

5. **Tune three camera/combat presentations around player decisions.** For sailing, preserve water ahead while exposing more of the hero deck in a slightly offset view. For broadside, keep the opponent's hull and water between the ships visible, with a clear firing-side muzzle flash, readable travel streak, and distinct impact splash. For crew, lower or shift the camera to frame the three work stations and crop less useful roof/sail area. Enlarge critical targeting and action text where needed. **Acceptance:** one can immediately identify own ship, enemy, firing side, shot destination, and crew jobs in the respective frames, while retaining room to steer and track threats.

## Can screenshots alone establish parity?

**No.** These screenshots are sufficient to reject a claim of current visual parity: the form, material, lighting, and world-detail gaps are directly visible. Even much stronger stills would establish only image-level quality at selected moments. Acceptance also needs ordinary gameplay footage and interactive inspection covering camera transitions, sail/crew movement, ship buoyancy, wake continuity, repeated cannon exchanges, collisions/occlusion, distant detail stability, and performance on intended hardware. None of those behaviors can be credited or condemned from these three stills alone.
