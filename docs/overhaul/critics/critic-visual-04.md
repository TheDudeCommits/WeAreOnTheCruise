**Verdict: 3/10 visual proximity to the supplied concepts. The requested theatrical, cinematic anime ship-battle spectacle is not yet achieved in these captures.** The world has a recognizable pirate-adventure identity, a strong painted cloudscape, saturated blue water, and distinct ship silhouettes. The visible ship, crew, environment, combat presentation, and materials remain substantially simpler than the concepts. Functional correctness cannot change this visual verdict.

This is an independent image-only review. I inspected all eight `docs/art-direction/concepts/*.png`, all eight `output/overhaul-gauntlet/pass-09/*.png`, `ui-09/desktop-embark.png`, and `aim-09/desktop-port-aim.png`. I did not read source code, tests, or earlier reports and did not use a browser. Scores are subjective art-direction judgments, not measured pixel similarity or an assessment of gameplay.

Runtime gives the ship less screen presence and places simple hillside buildings behind a large interface region. The difference is visible in both staging and asset quality.
- **02 — Hero ship / 03 — Living sea:** Sunny broadside, calm sailing, and embark show the intended recognizable vessel and blue-sea mood. The stern chase camera hides some bow detail, but the embark and crew-closeup angles expose enough of the model to confirm the missing layers of detail and material finish.

Nevertheless, the provided broadside image does not demonstrate the target spectacle, and the aim image does not remedy it. The visible enemy ships also lack the concept's hull depth, fittings, and sail complexity regardless of timing.
- **05 — Damage and defeat:** Runtime shows a damaged player ship rather than a defeated enemy. I do not treat that mismatch as proof that an enemy-defeat sequence is absent. I can judge the visible damage treatment, which is far less layered and physically specific than the reference.
- **06 — Crew at work:** The closeup and repair captures are strong enough correspondence to judge the visible character models, deck dressing, and work staging. These are major gaps, not unresolved camera questions.
- **07 — Arch passage:** Calm sailing shows the large arch, but from a different approach and crop. A matching wide shot would help composition assessment. It would not resolve the visibly plain cliff construction or sparse settlement and vegetation.
- **08 — Next voyage:** No route-chart screenshot was included in this review set. Its specific UI flow is unassessed. The supplied embark frame can only support a judgment of the shared ship/world presentation and current interface aesthetic.

Storm and night have no full-size matched concepts here. They demonstrate weather and palette variation, but tinting the same sparse geometry does not by itself add cinematic drama. Still images cannot establish animation quality, motion timing, camera feel, performance, or whether uncaptured effects exist.

## The three highest-impact engine/asset actions

1. **Build one authored hero-ship and opposing-ship asset pair to the target finish, with a small expressive crew.** Prioritize hull curvature and depth, recessed gunports and portholes, layered trim and deck levels, designed cannon carriages, materially convincing rigging, constructed cloth, and a fuller sculpted figurehead. Add a compact set of authored plank/fabric/metal textures and a cohesive toon lighting treatment with contact shadows and restrained edge definition. Replace the visible simplified crew with a few well-shaped, rigged characters carrying tools or ammunition and posing against actual stations. Judge a close three-quarter ship view and deck work vignette before extending that quality across the fleet. Increasing the ship count or adding more primitive pieces will not close this gap as effectively.

2. **Create one densely authored harbor-and-arch environment, and make the ocean visibly interact with it and the ships.** Replace the dominant pale block cliffs with layered rock silhouettes, ledges, foliage groups, settlement clusters, a real waterfront, and a waterfall that reads at gameplay distance. Give the water a controlled hierarchy of broad stylized wave planes, smaller broken crest foam, and persistent bow/stern interaction: wraparound hull foam, displaced spray, wake trails, and island-edge surf. Keep the excellent cloud direction, but bring the foreground geometry and material language up to its visual specificity. Acceptance image: the arch fills the scene as a rich place, while the ship visibly cuts through the water.

3. **Author a battle presentation system that stages a readable visual event.** Use three-quarter combat framing that keeps the player battery and target visible at meaningful scale, with deliberate camera support for the firing and impact moments. Build a connected sequence of staggered muzzle flashes, expanding smoke with distinct silhouettes, readable projectile trails, tall directional water impacts, splinters, localized fires, torn/broken ship parts, debris, and a visible defeat posture. Aiming should establish where the battery is and what it can hit without a large text panel becoming the central subject. Capture a timed salvo-to-impact sequence and a damaged closeup; the requirement is a compelling visible event, not an FX counter or a passing input test.

The nautical interface can be given illustrated framing and stronger icon art after these three changes, but interface decoration will not compensate for the current scene gap. There is a promising palette and recognizable identity here; the supplied evidence supports an early stylized 3D presentation, not a close concept match or AAA visual approval.
