## Concrete visual findings

- **Portrait embark:** Queen Mama Chanter's subtitle extends below the bottom edge of its card into the gap above the selected-ship detail. Several ship descriptions wrap into three lines inside short cards. This is local content overflow even though the receipt reports no page-wide horizontal overflow. The buttons and Plan Your Voyage remain visible.
- **Portrait embark:** bright sky, sail, and island shapes show through the ship grid. The first-row subtitles and the Ship No. 01 line have poor contrast. The nine options are identifiable, but comparing their supporting labels takes effort. Landscape embark removes the selected ship's stats and special description from the visible entry screen, leaving less information for choosing a vessel.
- **Portrait helm:** the Navigator label and “Choose your first route” subtitle run through the left directional-control area. The instruction and touch targets compete for the same pixels; this is an actual overlap, not merely crowded styling. AIM R is isolated on a fourth row while the other nine action buttons occupy three rows, making the paired aim controls harder to discover.
- **All helm sizes:** the lower Navigator message says “Choose your first route” while the upper objective already says to reach the wreck and hold within 65 m for 12 seconds. The desktop and landscape voyage status also names The Wreck Channel. This gives two different next actions in the same active state.
- **Portrait helm/crew-orders:** pale mission text, voyage label, and compass are laid over the bright stone arch and partly disappear. The desktop and landscape active helm have a dark-backed route/risk/banked line; the portrait active image does not visibly provide that line.
- **Pause/settings:** the main chart and return actions are prominent. Keyboard help is very small in all sizes and especially difficult at native phone width. Portrait settings remap rows have clearer labels than the static help legend.
- **Crew menus:** selection and allocation are readable. The desktop/portrait quick menu shows every preset; the landscape quick menu shows the first three and part of Repair, with Special below the fold. A scrollbar is visible. The landscape version omits the explanatory reload sentence from its visible top portion.

## Clipping versus accessibility limits

The short landscape layouts intentionally scroll: contract builds and Accept Contract, lower crew presets, restored Back to the Helm, and parts of pause/settings lie below the screenshot edge. Portrait contracts also puts Accept Contract below the fold. Visible scrollbars mean these are **not proven inaccessible**. The receipt demonstrates progressing through the route flow but does not independently establish touch scrolling, all lower settings, or every offscreen action's reachability. Do not count ordinary screenshot cropping at a scroll boundary as a broken action. In contrast, the portrait ship subtitle overflow and helm subtitle/control overlap are visible defects.

The receipt reports no JavaScript/shader errors and no page-wide horizontal overflow at all sizes. Those checks do not establish contrast, local text fit, control occlusion, combat usability, or actual touch-device behavior. Restoration is a supplied runtime assertion; the screenshots show a restored leg-one state and retained Gunnery selection, but cannot independently prove every saved field or completed-voyage continuity.

## Top three fixes

1. **Repair the portrait composition.** Give ship cards sufficient text height or shorten the subtitles; provide reliable dark backing for mission/compass text; reserve a separate subtitle strip outside the touch controls; keep AIM L/AIM R adjacent. Show compact active at-risk/banked values on portrait. Re-capture at 390×844 with the same bright arch background.
2. **Make guidance match the active state and input method.** Replace “Choose your first route” as soon as the wreck route begins. Explain the touch arrows, aim hold behavior, firing, and repair in readable phone-sized help. Keep help consistent with saved remaps. Retain the current clear primary chart/return actions.
3. **Explain the consequence of each voyage and crew decision, then prove the missing states.** Add concise crew outcome summaries beside the hand counts and state when unbanked coins are lost versus secured. Capture a nonzero reward/extraction choice, bank/refit result, restored earned state, plus aim/bearing and reload-in-progress views. The present route reward labels are a good foundation, but the supplied zero-coin READY-only snapshots cannot close tasks 4 and 8.
