## 5. Persistent damage and a playable aftermath

Ships progress through intact, scarred, critical, disabled, sinking and sunk presentation. Hull breaches, sail deformation, fire and flood list respond to authoritative damage. Disabled targets remain visible and available instead of disappearing when an encounter ends. Within 240 m, an appropriately credited target offers salvage, spare or scuttle. Each choice resolves and rewards once; salvage eventually sinks the emptied hull, spare preserves it, and scuttle starts sinking immediately.

Objective completion leaves a navigable aftermath until the player explicitly collects the encounter reward. This gives time to approach a disabled enemy and watch its resolution. The player's own loss still matters during aftermath and forfeits the unbanked voyage reward. Finish state, sinking time, damage, projectiles and pending gun queues survive save/restore.

## 6. Crew as a tactical resource

Ten hands are distributed across helm, guns, repair and special stations. Balanced, Gunnery, Sailing, Repair and Special presets expose their station counts and tradeoffs; the direct Crew Orders menu safely pauses interaction. Allocation changes speed/turning, reload, repair output and special charge. Four or more repair hands enable automatic repairs when not bracing; holding R requests repairs with other allocations. Repair activity also has handling/fire tradeoffs.

Moving hands between stations rescales the remaining reload duration while preserving completed reload work, preventing a fast-gunnery/instant-repair exploit. Visible figures walk to role stations and perform helm, gun, repair or rally poses. The latest original procedural crew adds shaped jackets, shoulders, hands, boots and face/hair volumes within the existing batched construction and animation pivots.

Source: [crew presets/build modifiers](../../src/content/voyages.ts), [allocation and reload semantics](../../src/simulation/GameSimulation.ts), [crew geometry/poses](../../src/render/ships/ShipGeometryFactory.ts). The game does not contain downloaded or production-rigged One Piece crew models.

## 7. Ship-specific specials

The [original approved scope](APPROVED-SCOPE.md) makes this a separate work package. Build 14 includes distinct effects and mechanics for the three reference vessels. All specials have authoritative wind-up, active and recovery phases; disabling the ship interrupts commitment. The existing special meter shows the phase and remaining time, while enemy target intent surfaces special commitment for counterplay. [Target art](../art-direction/concepts/09-ship-specials.png) is generated; the [special comparison](gallery/index.html#section-07) uses an actual cinematic Moby front, its overhead view at the same paused instant, and separate Sunny and Polar captures.

- **Sunny burst:** gold stern charge resolves into multiple aft pressure jets and exhaust. The burst changes motion and creates a temporary break in the connected wake. Wind-up is 0.65 seconds, active 1.2 and recovery 1.25.
- **Polar dive:** active buoyancy targets a physical 17 m descent with a surface disturbance and a detached submerged wake. The 3.2-second dive blocks every battery and cancels pending launches. Recovery lasts at least 1.25 seconds, then remains held until every actual scheduled gun mount clears its locally sampled water by more than 0.1 m. Its timer clamps during this hold; the HUD says SURFACING and the battery cards remain unavailable. Phase release means a fresh fire input can launch. This is physical submergence, without a claim of invulnerability.
- **Moby pressure wave:** a 1.05-second telegraph precedes a front expanding to 135 m over 1.8 seconds from a fixed world origin. Damage occurs once per target when the advancing front crosses its actual motion and collision path; the persisted hit ledger prevents repeated hits. The rendered crest follows the sampled sea surface and uses the authoritative radius. Recovery lasts 1.25 seconds. It is no longer an immediate area-damage event with a decorative ring.

## 9. Voyages, replay choices and recovery (additional replayability work)

The harbor now connects to three contracts and three build identities. Each contract has three legs with two seeded route offers per leg, culminating in a captain battle. Encounters include battle, salvage, storm, escort and boss. Six temporary rewards offer benefits with costs or immediate supplies; three permanent harbor refits spend banked coins. Earned objectives unlock explicit collection, reward selection and early extraction. Losing the ship preserves previously banked coins but forfeits the current unbanked haul.

Payout resolution, the paid-voyage ledger and progression share a versioned save. Restoring validates finite values, canonical contracts/routes/rewards, ship and voyage identities, gate geometry and progress, timers, projectiles, runtime and payout consistency before applying anything. Invalid restore is a no-op. Build 10 corrects a real browser floating-point accumulator underflow that previously rejected an otherwise ordinary autosave. Rejected bytes are quarantined under `cruise.voyage.recovery.v1` before fresh autosaves; if recovery storage fails, saves are blocked to preserve the primary record. Normal sessions use `cruise.voyage.v1`; scenario/debug URLs are isolated from that session.

A minimal persistent Sunwatch rival record tracks encounters, escapes and defeats and changes the returning boss hull/role after an escape. It is a small replay mechanic, not a complete nemesis director, mastery system, daily challenge service or balanced campaign. Content breadth and the value of repeated human sessions remain open.
