## Ship-by-ship findings

### Thousand Sunny — identity 8/10, target proximity 5/10

All five Sunny images were reviewed: ready, windup, active, recovery, and recovered.

The lion figurehead, cream emblem sail, orange secondary sail, red/gold hull, striped dome, and green deck features make Sunny recognizable without relying on the ship label. The ready and recovered angles present that identity clearly. Rigging and small crew silhouettes add useful scale.

The hull has broad flat bands and sparse ports, the foliage reads as polygon balls, and the figurehead and sail emblem are very simplified compared with the target's contour work and layered fittings. The visual mass is less substantial than the target's tall, richly detailed ship. The target's strong warm material separation, dark recesses, and local highlights are missing across large areas.

The active screenshot clearly shows three stern jets. Their direction and separation match the burst idea, but they appear as long, straight cream/gold ribbons extending off the frame across the lower-right HUD. The image lacks the dense white water, churning wake, spray, and energetic water contact that carry the target's burst. Recovery leaves thin white streaks and pale oval pieces, which read as discrete shapes rather than a dissipating foamy wake. Windup is visually weak away from its `CHARGING` label.

### Polar Tang — identity 6/10, target proximity 3.5/10

All six Polar images were reviewed: ready, windup, active, recovery, surfacing, and surfaced.

Yellow hull, periscope, red tower cap, side portholes, and the dark circular emblem distinguish the submarine from the sailing ships. The capsule silhouette is readable. However, its largely smooth elongated hull, plank-like faceting, minimal upper structure, and flat fin look much simpler than the rounded mechanical submarine in the target.

The active view makes a dive understandable through the missing hull, exposed periscope, bubbles, small foam ring, and `SUBMERGED` weapon labels. It does **not** reproduce the target's main visual payoff: the large yellow vessel visibly suspended beneath a translucent cyan surface. The hull is not discernible below the water in this frame. Consequently, the active subject shrinks to a tiny marker amid a mostly empty ocean.

Surfacing has greater visual emphasis, but its pale aqua/cream particles resemble overlapping flat stones or puffs around the tower. They need watery edges, sheets, and irregular spray to read as displacement. The recovered hull is legible in the surfaced frame, although the rival ship on the left becomes a strong competing mass. Ready and windup are close enough visually that their difference depends primarily on the HUD and a few surrounding shapes.

### Moby Dick — identity 4.5/10, target proximity 4/10

All seven Moby images were reviewed: ready, windup, active, wave-expanded, wave-cinematic, recovery, and recovered.

The overhead set shows a large multi-masted sailing ship, but hides the whale bow and makes the sail emblem difficult to read. In those views, its most distinctive identity is the `Moby Dick` label. The cinematic view reveals the large pale bow feature and dark sail graphic, yet the bow reads as a broad, flattened, faceted form with large angular appendages rather than the target's sculpted whale head with clear facial anatomy. This is the weakest specific ship identity in the evidence.

The windup arcs, small active ring, expanded ring, translucent recovery ring, and recovered absence provide the strongest still-image sequence of staged effects in this set. The close cinematic capture confirms that the expanded shape has apparent height, turquoise volume, and a white crest; it is visibly more than a flat targeting circle.

Even so, it resembles a near-perfect circular raised wall around the ship. The target uses a large ocean swell with irregular crest, weight, spray, and a close relationship to the whale bow. The ring's smooth regularity and repeating surface pattern make it feel procedural. From overhead, its turquoise edge is also competing against a dense field of white ocean speckles. The cinematic frame improves effect visibility but has cropped/overlapping neighboring ships at the left edge and does not isolate a clean hero silhouette.

## Prioritized visible issues

1. **P1 — Unify the subject rendering with the finished sky.** The hero ships and islands remain visibly faceted and under-detailed beside the elaborate painted clouds. Prioritize stronger ship silhouettes, purposeful dark recesses, material variation, and selected contour/detail work. More effect brightness alone will not close this central target gap. Evidence: all ready views, Sunny recovered, Moby wave-cinematic.
2. **P1 — Give Polar's dive a visible submerged subject.** In the active frame the yellow hull is absent and the effect becomes a small periscope marker. Recover the target's underwater yellow mass, depth tint, coherent surface opening, and vertical bubbles while keeping its position readable. Evidence: Polar active, compared directly with the middle target panel.
3. **P1 — Make all three effects look like water or propulsion acting on water.** Sunny's rigid light ribbons need a substantial disturbed wake; Polar's disks need irregular splash shapes; Moby's regular ring needs organic crest structure and spray. Preserve their already distinct directional, submerged, and radial signatures. Evidence: Sunny active/recovery, Polar surfacing, Moby wave-cinematic.
4. **P1 — Resolve Moby's whale bow silhouette and hero view.** Its defining shape is concealed overhead and overly abstract in the close capture. A clear head profile, facial features, and dimensional hull integration would materially improve identity. Use a composition that keeps the bow readable without adjacent ship overlaps. Evidence: Moby ready and wave-cinematic.
5. **P2 — Strengthen readable anticipation outside the HUD.** Sunny and Polar windups remain close to their ready silhouettes. Moby has visible arcs, but these look like graphical strips. Give each windup a clear, ship-local visual focus. This recommendation concerns the captured poses; the timing of anticipation is unverified.
6. **P2 — Reduce background competition and increase meaningful environmental depth.** The ocean's repeated small white marks dominate Moby's overhead frames, while the islands are sparse pale terraces. Larger coherent wave forms, controlled quiet areas around subjects, and richer cliff/vegetation grouping would bring the scene nearer the targets. Evidence: Moby overhead sequence and Sunny active.
7. **P2 — Improve special status hierarchy and capture readability.** Special states are shown in a tiny footer line beneath three weapon cards, while the cards repeat state words during Polar's dive. Some text is clipped (`SUBMERGE…`, target ship names), and the burst crosses the same HUD region. All three ready captures also show an extremely faint HUD; that is a visible evidence limitation, not a diagnosed UI bug.

## Evidence boundary

These stills demonstrate differing appearances at named capture points. They do not establish smooth transitions, actual acceleration, dive invulnerability, collision changes, seaquake damage or knockback, cooldown accuracy, input responsiveness, camera quality in motion, sound, frame rate, or performance stability. `DIRECT HIT`, speed values, `SUBMERGED`, and other HUD messages are visible UI assertions, not independent proof of those mechanics. The `VOYAGE PAUSED` label appears in the captures, so they should not be presented as evidence of continuous play or fluid motion.

**Disposition:** visually recognizable special concepts, with the sky/palette closest to target; substantial hero-asset and effect work remains before cinematic anime target parity can be claimed.
