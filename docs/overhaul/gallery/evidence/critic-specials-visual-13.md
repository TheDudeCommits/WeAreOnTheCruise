# Blind visual review — specials 13

Reviewed only the two target PNGs and all 17 candidate PNGs in `output/overhaul-gauntlet/specials-13/`. No implementation, JSON, receipts, handover, prior criticism, or browser was read. Scores concern visible still-image evidence; they do not assess actual timing, continuity, controls, damage, or frame rate.

**Verdict: the three special concepts are distinguishable at a broad level, but cinematic anime target quality is not met. Overall target parity: 3/10. Special identity separation: 6/10.**

The strongest match is the blue-and-cream cloud palette. Sunny has a recognizable colorful sailing-ship silhouette; Polar is clearly a yellow submarine; Moby has a much larger multi-sail silhouette. The special vocabulary differs—stern trail, disappearance below water, expanding ring—but its rendering is substantially simpler and less forceful than the target's jets, underwater reveal, and foaming pressure wave.

## Priority 1 — Make the defining physical effects unmistakable

1. **Sunny needs a visible stern-powered burst, with readable preparation and aftermath.** In `thousand-sunny-windup.png`, the distinguishing effect is a tiny pair of pale arcs beside the stern; the vessel looks almost like the ready frame. In `thousand-sunny-active.png`, a short pale fan protrudes from behind the right side of the stern, mostly hidden by the hull. There are no clearly separated luminous nozzle cores or long energetic jets like the target. In recovery, the remaining white streak is thin and generic. Show the stern outlets and shape a larger directional burst with a bright core, separated streams, water spray, and a foam track. Give windup a stronger stern-localized gathering cue and recovery a clearly dissipating burst trail. A rear three-quarter capture would also make the existing stern evidence much easier to judge.

2. **Polar needs visible immersion and emergence, rather than a disappearing hull.** `polar-tang-active.png` is mostly open sea with two thin mast/periscope elements at center; the target communicates the yellow submerged body through blue water, bubbles, and a circular disturbed surface. In `polar-tang-surfacing.png`, crew figures and the tower rise from the water with no substantial splash crown or sheeting water. In `polar-tang-surfaced.png`, the hull intersects the broad water surface without a convincing wet edge, displaced bow water, or draining foam. Crew feet are visibly separated from the hull in the ready/surfaced views, and in the surfacing image crew appear to stand in the ocean. Restore a clear relationship between crew, deck, and waterline; add a recognizable underwater silhouette, bubble column, shaped dive disturbance, and a substantial foaming emergence crest. The current smooth ribbon rings look drawn around the submarine rather than made from the sea.

3. **Moby needs a pressure wave with height, thickness, and breaking foam.** `moby-dick-wave-expanded.png` shows an almost perfect smooth white ellipse surrounding the vessel. `moby-dick-recovery.png` retains a broader, faded version of this outline. The water immediately inside and outside the ring remains visually similar, so the effect reads as a radius marker. The target has a thick turquoise wall, a rolling lip, turbulent white crest, and a clear direction of force. Replace the outline appearance with a visibly raised wave band, strong front/back value separation, irregular breaking crest, thrown spray, and a disturbed-water aftermath. The windup arcs can establish gathering pressure, but currently share the same clean ribbon vocabulary as Polar.

## Priority 2 — Bring the ships and sea into the target's visual language

4. **The hero assets remain visibly coarse next to the illustrated sky.** Sunny's broad flat hull sections, angular lion mane and dome, simplified railings, nearly blank planks, and faceted green tree balls are far below the target's layered fittings, panel linework, nuanced sail shading, and dense but readable deck detail. Polar is especially sparse: a simple yellow capsule with exposed facets, a red cap, minimal ports, and flat rectangular fins. The target uses convincing metal construction, dimensional portholes, articulated forms, and much richer shadow structure. Improve silhouettes, constructed surface detail, intentional ink/edge treatment, and localized shadows before adding more scene-wide effects.

5. **Water contact and effect materials lack weight.** Sunny's bow and much of its hull have only a narrow contact line rather than the target's energetic bow displacement. Polar's hull clips into broad undifferentiated swell shapes. In Moby's top-down images the ocean is covered with repeated diagonal rows of foam flecks and sparkle, which compete with the special's ring. Give each hero a strong, localized water interaction and simplify nearby surface noise so the special's crest and turbulent aftermath become the dominant water shapes.

6. **Moby's capture angle hides its defining ship identity and the wave's volume.** The overhead view gives a useful radius comparison but reduces the ship to stacked white sails and a brown hull, losing the target's whale-shaped bow and imposing scale. The nearby enemies look like small simplified tokens. Keep an overhead evidence frame if useful, but also capture a closer low or medium three-quarter view that exposes the whale bow, wave lip, spray, and relative enemy scale. The supplied stills cannot establish the intended cinematic Moby identity from the hull view available.

## Priority 3 — Improve visual evidence and focus

7. **The centered “VOYAGE PAUSED” panel covers the special's focal area.** It overlays Sunny's central rigging, Polar's periscope/emergence location, and Moby's upper hull. Several ready images also have faint HUD layers. A clean evidence pass without the centered banner would make anticipation and impact silhouettes clearer. The active images should communicate the special before a viewer reads state labels or speed numbers.

8. **The presentation lacks the target's composed scale and lighting.** The candidate sky has an attractive anime color language, but the plain stepped islands, evenly lit primitive ship surfaces, and thin effect ribbons pull the scene toward an early stylized prototype. The target's directional shadows, dense ship construction, dramatic water shapes, and close subject framing need to agree as one visual treatment.
