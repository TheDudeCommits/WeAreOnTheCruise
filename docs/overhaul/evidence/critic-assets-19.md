## Match to target A

Target A combines crisp contour definition, warm material highlights, deliberately shaped shadows, rich but controlled color, readable crew activity, and dense localized foam where the hull displaces large waves. Its island architecture and cliffs support an authored composition.

These screenshots combine a detailed illustrated sky with visibly low-poly islands, smoother 3D ship shading, inconsistent material detail, and a glossy ocean covered in thin white surface traces. That mixture is conspicuous. The reference's sense of mass, sunlight, scale, and life is substantially stronger. Battle effects need not appear in a calm exploration shot, but the underlying material finish and convincing hull-to-water relationship should survive that change of scene.

## Top three actionable blockers

1. **Finish the weakest ship surfaces and bring all six into one anime shading treatment.** Prioritize Moby Dick's bow definition and pale-on-pale hull, Polar Tang's faceted body and visually disconnected stern element, and Baratie's fish and broad blank platforms. Improve navy cannon materials and Sunny's dull trim. Use controlled warm/cool shadow bands and consistent edge definition; preserve Merry's stronger wood/cloth/trim separation.
2. **Make hulls visibly displace the ocean.** Establish a clear immersion line, localized bow foam, side turbulence, darker contact water, and wakes that broaden and dissolve into the surrounding waves. Replace the obvious straight wake strips seen behind Merry and Polar Tang. Match the foam's scale and graphic character to the sky and target A, instead of relying on fine surface scribbles and isolated puffs.
3. **Frame each vessel around its actual silhouette and readable deck life.** Pull Baratie back enough to retain both platforms and mast tops; give the navy mast more headroom and rotate enough to reveal hull and deck rather than stacked sail faces. Keep crew visible at meaningful ship stations and large enough to read. Preserve a full-ship inspection angle as evidence, then use a lower, more intentional hero angle for cinematic presentation.

**Conclusion:** Recognizable ships are present. Finished fleet-wide visual quality and target-A cohesion are not yet demonstrated; these stills do not warrant acceptance.
